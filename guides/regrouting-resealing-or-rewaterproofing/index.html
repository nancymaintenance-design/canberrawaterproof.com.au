Regrouting, Resealing or Rewaterproofing: What’s the Difference?

A practical explanation of three different scopes that should not be treated as interchangeable.

What to look for

Start with observable information: the area affected, when it happens, any recent changes and the extent of visible moisture or deterioration. This helps frame a service request without treating the symptoms as a diagnosis.

Why the scope matters

Waterproofing, sealing, tiling, plumbing and drainage each require a focused repair pathway. MEL ONE reviews the source of entry and existing construction to recommend a clear, practical solution.

Prepare for a useful conversation

Include your Canberra suburb, affected area, timing and clear photos where available. A written scope should distinguish inspection, local repair, removal, waterproofing, retiling and finishing work.

What you can observe

Record where water appears, when it occurs, whether it follows shower use or rain, and whether any joints, finishes or surrounding surfaces have changed. These observations help define the discussion without treating them as a diagnosis.

How repair scopes differ

Surface sealing, plumbing repairs, drainage work, waterproofing replacement and retiling are separate tasks. A clear work scope names the affected area, access, preparation, materials, finishing work and any related trade coordination.

What to record before you request service

  • Your ACT suburb or locality
  • The affected room or external area
  • When the issue occurs and what you can see
  • Photos showing the affected detail and surrounding finishes
  • Previous repairs or renovation information where known
