Shower repair planning

Regrouting, resealing and rewaterproofing work on different parts of a shower. A clean new joint may improve a worn finish, but the right repair depends on where water is escaping and what remains sound. This guide helps Canberra property owners compare the proposed work, understand epoxy grout and silicone, and recognise when a leak needs investigation before another surface treatment.

Three Shower Repairs with Different Boundaries

Regrouting renews material between tiles within the agreed area. Resealing renews selected sealant joints, such as appropriate exposed junctions or screen interfaces. Rewaterproofing works on the waterproofing system, often with removal and reinstatement of finishes to obtain access. The words should not be used interchangeably in a quote.

Imagine three apparently similar showers. One has worn grout but no reported water escaping. Another sends water past a screen edge during use. A third causes dampness in an adjoining room despite recent surface repairs. The first needs a condition assessment of the finish; the second needs the escape route checked; the third needs the leak source reconsidered. Appearance alone cannot establish the concealed membrane condition in any of them.

Before comparing prices, make sure the proposed jobs address the same outcome. A quote to renew grout is not equivalent to a quote that removes tiles, repairs the substrate and reinstates waterproofing.

Grout, Silicone and Membrane Roles

Grout fills the joints within the tile finish. Silicone is a flexible sealant used at suitable joints. The membrane forms a separate waterproofing layer within the wet-area construction. Sika's sheet membrane system lists membrane, detailing components, adhesive and grout separately. That distinction matters when a surface repair is described as waterproofing.

A split sealant joint may allow unwanted water entry at that location. Replacing it does not inspect or reconstruct every concealed junction. Similarly, visible grout loss is a maintenance concern, but it does not prove that the underlying membrane has failed. The surrounding assembly and actual leak path still need assessment.

If a quote offers “full shower sealing”, ask exactly which joints and products it covers. Request a clear statement about whether the work is confined to the tile surface or includes the underlying waterproofing. The phrase alone tells you little about the investigation, access or construction being repaired.

Epoxy Grout and Cement-Based Grout

Epoxy and cement-based grouts are different product families, with suitability determined by the selected product and site conditions. ARDEX describes EG 15 epoxy grout for applications needing hygiene and chemical or physical resistance, and flags special procedures for some tile surfaces. A product's strengths do not make it a universal answer to an unexplained leak.

The Ellis team offers epoxy grout work. Choosing it still requires checking the existing tiles, joint condition and preparation needed. Ask which product is proposed, why it suits the tile finish, and how residue and appearance will be managed. A useful comparison discusses the actual application rather than treating “epoxy” as a promise that any shower can be repaired from above.

If water continues to reach another room, the repair decision extends beyond grout selection. Epoxy in the tile joints cannot reconnect a leaking pipe or reconstruct an inaccessible membrane termination. Those possibilities must remain part of the assessment until the evidence supports a narrower scope.

Shower Movement Joints and Membrane Bond Breaker Details

Rigid tile joints and flexible junctions have different purposes. Where an exposed corner has repeatedly cracked or opened, a professional should check how the junction is intended to accommodate movement. Simply replacing the visible material with a harder product does not explain why the joint opened.

A bond breaker is a detail within a waterproofing system that allows the membrane to accommodate movement across a junction. It is not another name for the visible silicone bead on the finished tiles. ARDEX explains the purpose and different system approaches in its bond breaker guidance. Its SE sealant page also distinguishes sealant applications and the product specified beneath its membranes.

A photo of the finished corner cannot confirm that the concealed detail was installed correctly. If reconstruction is proposed, ask how the junction will be detailed as part of the chosen system. Avoid a generic instruction to put any silicone under any membrane; compatibility and the system specification matter.

Old Shower Silicone Removal and Sealant Joint Preparation

A thick new bead over loose or contaminated old silicone may make a joint look renewed while leaving the original adhesion problem unresolved. When sealant is peeling at an edge, the visible separation gives a location to inspect. It does not establish whether water has already affected the surrounding construction or whether sealing that joint will resolve the entire leak.

Ask what removal, cleaning and drying the proposed repair includes. Sika's sealant preparation guidance requires a sound, clean, dry surface free of contaminants, including old sealants. The Australian project still needs a suitable locally specified product and its own current instructions; that example is not a product recommendation for every shower.

Preparation should also account for fragile edges, existing damage and access. Removing material aggressively can disturb the finish or deeper layers. The service description should identify which joints are being renewed and what happens if removal reveals an unsuitable surface. These are practical scope questions for shower resealing and regrouting.

A Shower Still Leaking After Regrouting

A shower still leaking after regrouting needs a review of the diagnosis and the work completed. Record whether the symptom is identical, smaller, larger or in a new location. Note whether the shower was returned to use according to the repair instructions, and keep the product and scope information. A recurrence does not, by itself, prove either poor workmanship or membrane failure.

Possible explanations include a source outside the grouted joints, an unresolved junction, plumbing, or concealed waterproofing defects. Previously wet materials can also complicate interpretation of what is seen after a repair. An inspection should distinguish ongoing water entry from damage that has not yet been assessed or restored.

Pause use if it is causing further damage and have the relevant trade reassess the source. The plumbing and waterproofing leak guide explains useful observations. A further coating or another bead of sealant should have an explained purpose and defined boundary, rather than be the automatic response to a failed first attempt.

Partial Repair and Rewaterproofing Limits

A local repair may be appropriate when the defect and repair boundary are understood. For example, a confirmed exposed seal failure can justify a defined joint repair. However, a small area of visible damage is not proof that the required waterproofing repair can remain equally small. The condition of the substrate and the ability to connect to sound existing waterproofing may affect the scope.

Where tiles need removal, ask how access will be opened, which fixtures may be affected and what the proposal assumes about the remaining construction. A partial repair should explain the proposed connection to the retained system and the uncertainties that opening up may reveal. If that connection cannot be established, a wider scope may need consideration.

Rewaterproofing does not automatically mean renovating every part of the bathroom. Equally, keeping the work within the shower is an outcome to assess, not a promise to make from photographs. Bathroom waterproofing work should be scoped around the actual construction, access and restoration required.

Shower Grout and Sealant Curing with a Clear Handover

Return-to-use instructions depend on the products, application and conditions. A surface that feels dry is not a reliable substitute for the specified curing period. The ARDEX EG 15 data sheet distinguishes traffic and full-cure stages and notes temperature effects. Those figures belong to that product and stated conditions; they should not become a standard downtime promise for every repair.

Request written instructions identifying when the shower can be used and cleaned, and who to contact if water reappears. Where several products are involved, the handover needs to account for the whole repair sequence. Include any monitoring requested by the repairer, without deliberately stressing the shower or carrying out a home flood test.

Keep before-and-after photographs, the agreed scope, product details and findings together. For a cosmetic grout renewal, that records the finish work. For a leaking shower, it also preserves what was investigated and what remains outside the repair. Clear records help the next trade avoid repeating an assumption that the first job never established.

Related waterproofing questions

Does regrouting fix a leaking shower?

It may address a confirmed problem within the grouted finish, but it cannot be assumed to fix plumbing or concealed waterproofing defects. If water reaches another room or returns after regrouting, reassess the source before choosing another surface treatment.

Does silicone replace the waterproofing membrane?

No. Exposed silicone joints and the underlying waterproofing system have separate roles. Some systems specify compatible sealants within concealed details, but renewing the visible bead does not reconstruct or verify the membrane beneath the tiles.

Can epoxy grout avoid tile removal in every leaking shower?

No. Epoxy grout is a grout choice, not evidence that the hidden construction is sound. The Ellis team offers epoxy grout work, with the repair scope determined by the defect, tile condition and preparation required. Some leaks need plumbing repairs or access to the waterproofing system.
