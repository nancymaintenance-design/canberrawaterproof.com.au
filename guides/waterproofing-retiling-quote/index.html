Waterproofing quote planning

Measurements and photographs are not needed before first contact. You do not need to collect every item before asking about repair or installation requirements. Tile selection can wait: contact MEL ONE before choosing tiles.

A waterproofing and retiling quote should distinguish inspection, removal, substrate preparation and falls, membrane details, curing and agreed tests, tile supply and laying, flexible joints, reinstatement, waste and related trades. MEL ONE’s Ellis team assesses the site and plans the agreed work around its condition and your intended finish. Compare the scope first, then the total: a membrane-only amount and a complete bathroom rebuild describe different jobs.

Comparable waterproofing and retiling scopes

Begin with the same brief for every provider. Identify the affected shower, bathroom or balcony, the symptoms, and which fixtures or finishes you hope to retain. Ask each quote to mark work as included, excluded or dependent on inspection. A single line saying “waterproofing and tiling” can hide different assumptions about removal, tile supply and returning the room to use.

A local resealing quote should name the accessible joints and preparation. Membrane renewal needs to explain how the concealed system will be reached, replaced and covered again. A complete waterproofing and retiling scope joins those stages with the finished surfaces and reinstatement. Compare each proposal against the actual repair objective.

Our regrouting, resealing or rewaterproofing guide explains those repair boundaries. For wet-area renewal, see bathroom waterproofing and retiling. External drainage, thresholds and access also affect a balcony waterproofing scope.

Shower-only work and full bathroom renewal

A shower-only repair may be feasible, but its boundary needs assessment. The scope must address the substrate, adjoining waterproofing and how the repaired area connects to surrounding construction. Keeping the rest of the bathroom can affect access and reinstatement; the smaller visible area does not establish that the underlying detail is straightforward.

Tell MEL ONE which bath, vanity, screen, tiles or fittings you want to keep. Ask whether removal can affect them and who handles replacement if they cannot be reused. Where the assessment supports a broader scope, request an explanation of the condition or connection that makes that work necessary.

A full bathroom proposal should show the finished outcome as well as the membrane stage. Confirm the extent of wall and floor tiling, edges, flexible joints and any plumbing changes. For a laundry renovation, apply the same scope checklist while identifying appliance connections and the agreed handover between trades. The quote should describe the actual room and layout.

Owner-supplied tiles and finish selections

If you have already bought tiles for an ensuite or bathroom, share their product information, dimensions, thickness and available quantity. The team needs to review suitability, layout, preparation and installation compatibility before including them. A box count alone does not establish whether there is enough material for the proposed layout and cuts.

Ask who checks quantities, arranges delivery, stores the tiles and supplies any additional material. Identify trims and edge details as well as the main tile. If more tiles are needed, availability and batch or appearance differences may affect the finish, so resolve that selection before laying wherever possible.

You do not need to select tiles before first contact. MEL ONE can assess the site and discuss tile supply and selections as part of confirming the written scope. Distinguish the cost of supplying tiles from laying, adhesive, grout and flexible joints, even when those items appear together in the final proposal. This makes comparison and later changes easier to understand.

Concealed waterproofing conditions and written approval for changes

Accessible checks cannot reveal every concealed condition. MEL ONE checks the visible tile layers, substrate and earlier repairs on site, then agrees the access needed to investigate further. If removal is needed before the full condition can be known, the quotation should explain that limit and the initial work you are authorising.

If opening the area exposes damage or a drainage issue, use a clear variation sequence: explain the finding, describe the additional preparation or trade work, set out its cost and timing effect, then obtain written approval before proceeding with the additional scope. Keep that update with the original quote. A changed project should not be judged against an outdated list of inclusions.

Name the person who can approve changes, particularly for a rental or strata property. Agree how findings and photographs will be shared if that person is away. This helps prevent a pause on site becoming a rushed decision made by someone without authority to approve the work.

Membrane curing, inspections and readiness for tiling

The quote should name the selected system and the handover point before it is covered. Substrate condition, junctions and drainage details need attention as part of the construction. Ask which inspections or tests are included, who carries them out and how readiness for the next stage will be recorded.

Product instructions distinguish stages that can have different waiting requirements. The ARDEX WPM 002 datasheet, for example, gives separate guidance for tiling, curing and flood testing, and explains that temperature affects drying and curing. It also identifies details that may dry more slowly. This illustrates why a generic promise about the next day is inadequate; it does not specify the product for your job.

Weather and site conditions can affect the agreed sequence. Where testing is specified, it must suit the selected product and project. Do not fill a suspect bathroom or balcony to trial a flood test yourself. Agree how completed waterproofing will be protected from damage or later penetrations before other trades return.

Bathroom downtime in occupied homes, rentals and strata

Confirm room downtime before scheduling work: which facilities become unavailable, when removal starts, and which preparation, curing, testing and finishing steps must be complete before use resumes. Time spent applying a product is only one part of the period during which a room may be unavailable.

If this is your only bathroom, raise that at the first discussion. Ask whether the toilet, basin and shower will all be affected and plan alternative washing arrangements around the confirmed sequence. Do not assume keeping one fixture in place means residents can safely use it throughout the work.

For an occupied rental, agree entry, resident communication and the approval contact with the property manager. For an apartment, include lift bookings, permitted work hours, common-area protection and relevant strata arrangements. Give MEL ONE your suburb, floor level, parking and entry details. Ask how a delayed stage will be communicated so residents can adjust their arrangements without relying on an unconfirmed return date.

Waterproofing handover records and project-specific requirements

Request a handover record identifying the area treated, agreed materials, relevant photographs, included inspection or test records, care instructions and the point at which normal use can resume. List remaining reinstatement tasks and who is responsible for completing them. Keep the record with the quotation and approved variations for future maintenance.

The ABCB FAQ directs existing-building alterations to state or territory rules. Confirm the requirements for the ACT project and who provides any required documentation. A request for useful completion records should not be treated as a promise that a particular certificate will be supplied.

For an enquiry, provide the property type, affected room, symptoms, previous repairs and the finishes you hope to retain. Photos are optional; measurements are not required before first contact. Request a waterproofing and retiling scope with your suburb and a short description. The Ellis team checks the site, explains the findings and confirms the repair plan and written quote.

Related waterproofing questions

Can I compare waterproofing quotes using the total alone?

Compare the same removal, preparation, materials, finishes, related trades and reinstatement first. A membrane-only amount can exclude work needed to return the room to use. Ask each provider to identify inclusions, exclusions and conditions awaiting inspection.

Can I use tiles I have already bought?

Share the tile specifications, size, thickness and available quantity. Suitability and the installation scope need review before owner-supplied tiles are included. Confirm responsibility for additional tiles, delivery, trims and any changes to preparation or laying.

How long will my only bathroom be unavailable?

The period depends on the agreed work, selected products and site conditions. Confirm which facilities are affected, the sequence and the return-to-use requirements before booking. Arrange alternative facilities around that plan and agree how any changes will be communicated.
