External Waterproofing and Drainage Assessment in Canberra

Water beside a building and water entering a building are related observations, but they do not automatically require the same repair. Drainage work manages where water goes; waterproofing and sealing work address defined water-entry details. Some properties need both. Before choosing a coating, new drain or joint repair, establish when the moisture appears, where water gathers and which part of the building is affected. This guide helps Canberra owners and managers prepare that assessment without assuming every damp patch has the same cause.

The moisture pattern comes before the repair method

Describe the symptom in ordinary terms. Examples include a puddle against the wall after rain, a wet window reveal during windy weather, water crossing the back-door sill or dampness in a garage beside retained soil. These descriptions are useful because they locate the issue without naming an unconfirmed defect.

Record the first point at which you notice water and the time it appears. Distinguish active water from an old stain and note whether the surface dries between events. Include recent changes such as paving, landscaping, a door replacement or work to a nearby downpipe. An inspection can then compare the account with accessible building and site details.

Keep an open diagnosis where the evidence is incomplete. A wet wall after rain does not establish that the whole wall needs coating. A puddle near a door does not prove the door itself is defective. Ask for the findings, proposed cause and any uncertainty to be stated separately before agreeing to a construction method.

Water pooling and wall-base moisture

For water pooling beside the house, observe the wider route rather than only the puddle. From a safe location, note whether runoff seems to arrive from paving, a garden, a downpipe area or higher ground. Photograph the surrounding layout and any visible drain, then take a closer image of the wall base. Say how long the water remains after the rain stops.

This is information for an assessment, not an instruction to redirect water yourself. A proposed drainage change needs a suitable discharge route and consideration of the whole site. Ask the responsible drainage trade to explain the collection point, route and destination. A new channel alongside the wall is not a complete proposal if its discharge has not been established.

If paving or soil has recently been raised, identify that change in the enquiry. Do not excavate along the footing or remove ground against a wall to expose a suspected membrane. The inspection should establish what access is safe and whether drainage, building details or both require further investigation.

Window leaks during wind-driven rain

For a window that leaks only with rain from one direction, record the room, affected side and first visible wet point. Include photographs of the whole window and the interior reveal. Exterior photographs should be taken only from normal, safe access. Mention whether water appears around the frame, at the sill or higher on the wall.

The assessment should consider the window assembly and its connection to the surrounding wall. Ask whether the proposed work concerns an accessible perimeter joint, a flashing detail, the window itself or another part of the facade. That distinction determines who needs to inspect and what a sealing quote can reasonably include.

A blanket instruction to seal every opening is not a useful substitute for identifying the assembly. Tell the contractor about earlier attempts and request an explanation of which joints are to be repaired. Avoid applying sealant to unfamiliar slots or drainage openings. Where the cause remains concealed, agree the investigation and reinstatement scope before finishes are opened.

Back-door rainwater leaks and adjoining paving

Water at a back door deserves a view of both sides of the threshold. Record whether you see runoff approaching the opening, water sitting outside the sill or moisture first appearing inside the frame. Note whether the door was closed and whether wind seemed to drive rain towards it. These details help distinguish the reported event without prescribing a repair.

Ground-level paving beside a door is different from a balcony above an occupied room. The former may require review of site runoff and threshold details. The latter also needs consideration of the construction over the room below; see the balcony leak guide for that situation.

Ask how any proposed paving, drainage or threshold change will connect with the existing door and wall. If more than one trade is involved, identify the sequence and handover between them. The objective is a coordinated scope for the reported entry path, including the finishes that must be removed and restored to reach it.

Condensation, rain entry and persistent low-level damp

Not all indoor moisture arrives as liquid rain. The Australian Government's YourHome condensation guidance explains that condensation forms when humid air meets a cooler surface and can occur both visibly and within construction. A rain-entry repair therefore needs evidence that it addresses the reported source.

Record whether droplets appear on glass on dry mornings, whether moisture follows cooking or showering, and whether the problem also occurs without rain. These clues can prompt assessment of ventilation or building conditions. They do not rule out a separate leak: more than one issue can exist at a property.

Persistent dampness low on a wall also needs a careful description. Staining, salts or peeling paint alone are not enough for this guide to diagnose rising damp, groundwater or a failed barrier. YourHome's repairs and maintenance guidance identifies several possible moisture sources. Ask the assessor what evidence supports the diagnosis and whether additional building, plumbing or moisture investigation is needed before selecting treatment.

Drainage and waterproofing in the same repair plan

A useful proposal gives each task a purpose. Drainage work should describe the water it will collect or redirect and the intended discharge arrangement. Waterproofing or sealing work should identify the part of the building it treats, the access and preparation needed, and the detail it is intended to protect. Avoid comparing proposals solely by the area of coating or length of drain.

Sika Australia's waterproofing guidance treats system selection, substrate preparation and falls to drainage as distinct considerations. Manufacturer information supports planning around the assembly and conditions; it does not establish which product is suitable for an unseen property.

Where both types of work are proposed, ask who resolves the interfaces. A drain installed beside a wall and a waterproofed wall detail must be considered together in the agreed design. The external waterproofing service scope should identify the Ellis team's work and any separate drainage task so the owner can see how the overall repair will be completed.

Retaining walls and adjoining garage moisture

A garage wall beside retained soil needs more context than an exposed external wall. Tell the assessor which side retains ground, what access exists outside and where moisture first appears inside. Include available plans and previous work records. If the soil-facing side cannot be reached, state that early; access can materially affect the investigation and possible scope.

Do not assume an internal coating is equivalent to repairing the retained side. Ask the proposal to explain the chosen repair side, the intended outcome and any unresolved drainage or access issue. Retaining wall waterproofing needs a defined area and method, with excavation, drainage and protection work identified where relevant.

This guide cannot assess wall stability or diagnose structural movement. Report visible changes such as a newly opening crack, displaced finish or apparent movement, and arrange appropriate specialist assessment. Do not dig behind a retaining wall, remove supporting soil or drill investigation holes yourself. Structural questions should be resolved by the relevant specialist before relying on a waterproofing-only proposal.

External leak observations and coordinated waterproofing scope

Prepare a short record that another person can follow: the ACT address, property type, affected room or exterior area, weather timing, photographs and previous repairs. Include one wide photograph to show the layout rather than sending only close-ups of damp paint. Observe rain from shelter where practical; avoid roofs, ladders, flooded areas and unstable ground.

For managed residential or commercial property, identify the authorised contact, tenant access and any operating restrictions. Agree what the first visit covers and what additional investigation would require separate approval. Plumbing, roofing, window, drainage, waterproofing and structural tasks may need different input. Each task should have an identified owner, sequence and reinstatement allowance.

MEL ONE's Ellis team assesses external water-ingress work across Canberra and the ACT for residential, strata and commercial properties. Request a written scope that connects the evidence to the proposed work, lists separate trade requirements and explains how unexpected findings will be handled. That gives you a practical basis for choosing the repair and coordinating access.

Related waterproofing questions

Does water pooling beside my house mean I need waterproofing?

Not automatically. The assessment should establish where the water comes from, where it can discharge and whether it is entering a building detail. The scope may require drainage, waterproofing or coordinated work by different trades.

Why does my window leak only in windy rain?

The weather pattern is useful evidence, but does not identify the defect on its own. Record the direction of the rain and the first visible wet point. An assessment should distinguish the window assembly, perimeter joint and adjoining wall or flashing details before proposing sealing.

Can I waterproof a damp garage retaining wall from the inside?

The repair side and method depend on the wall construction, water path and available access. An internal treatment should not be assumed to address retained-side drainage or structural issues. Ask for the intended outcome, limitations and separate specialist tasks to be explained in the scope.
