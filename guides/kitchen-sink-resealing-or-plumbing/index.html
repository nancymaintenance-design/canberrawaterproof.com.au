Kitchen leak planning

Water in the cupboard under a kitchen sink does not identify the failed part. An accessible sink-edge seal may need replacement, but a supply connection, waste fitting or appliance can leave water in the same cupboard. Start with the timing and first visible wet point, then agree an assessment that separates sealing from plumbing and cabinet restoration. MEL ONE’s Ellis team assesses the affected detail on site and confirms the proposed work in writing.

Sink perimeter leaks and plumbing leak paths

A sink perimeter joint sits between the sink and benchtop. A splashback joint sits where the benchtop meets the wall finish. Either can let surface water reach an adjoining gap when the joint has failed. That is a different repair from a leaking tap connection, supply hose, waste assembly or pipe. A bead of silicone around the sink cannot repair those plumbing components.

Water can also travel along a surface before dripping. The lowest wet corner of the cupboard may be some distance from the starting point. Treat stains and pooled water as clues for inspection, rather than proof that the nearest joint needs sealing. Tell the assessor whether water follows bench cleaning, ordinary sink use, draining a full bowl or an appliance cycle. These patterns help organise the investigation; they do not establish a diagnosis by themselves.

For an accessible joint repair, the relevant service is kitchen sealing and resealing. A confirmed supply or waste fault needs plumbing assessment and its own repair scope.

Useful observations before a kitchen leak inspection

You can contact MEL ONE without photographs or measurements. If the area is safely accessible, a short description of the first place you noticed water is useful. Note whether the benchtop was wet, whether the tap or sink had recently been used, and whether the dishwasher or another connected appliance was operating. Existing photographs taken from a normal standing position can help explain the layout.

Keep observations passive. Do not deliberately flood the bench, fill the cupboard area, loosen fittings or pull an appliance out to reproduce a leak. Do not crawl into a damaged cabinet or reach near wet electrical fittings. If a leak is active, stop using the affected fixture where practical and arrange appropriate help. Active water near electricity needs prompt attention from the relevant professional.

A simple note such as “the cupboard was wet before breakfast, with no sink use since last night” gives more useful context than a guess about the membrane. Include earlier repairs and whether the wet patch is spreading.

Kitchen moisture appearing while the sink is unused

Water found while the sink is unused deserves a broader check than the visible sink edge. A water supply connection can remain pressurised when the tap is closed. An appliance may also run independently of sink use. Water remaining from an earlier event can take time to become visible, so an overnight wet patch does not prove a particular source.

Report when the cupboard was last seen dry, when you found the moisture, and whether nearby appliances were running. Distinguish fresh droplets you can already see from an old stain or swollen panel. Do not disconnect hoses, dismantle a trap or attempt isolation repairs to investigate. The person assessing the plumbing can decide what controlled checks are appropriate.

If the accessible perimeter looks intact but the cupboard keeps getting wet, adding more surface silicone may delay the correct repair. Ask for the suspected water path and the limits of the initial inspection to be explained before approving work.

Swollen cabinet repair after the moisture source is addressed

Resealing does not restore swollen, softened or delaminated cabinet board. It can address a confirmed joint leak, but the damaged material remains a separate issue. Replacing the board while the source is unresolved risks exposing the new material to the same water. Establish the leak path first, agree the repair, then assess what cabinet work is needed.

Tell the team whether a shelf has sagged, a door no longer closes, or a panel has pulled away from an adjoining surface. Avoid loading damaged shelves or disturbing a sink support. These observations help the assessor consider access and whether the surrounding construction can support the proposed repair.

Ask the written scope to distinguish source repair, any opening needed to reach it, drying or further condition checks, and cabinet restoration. A sealing quote should not imply that it includes replacing a cabinet carcass or remaking a benchtop unless those tasks are expressly listed. Keep photographs and the agreed scope together for later follow-up.

Split kitchen splashback silicone and recurring joint movement

A split at the splashback-to-benchtop junction is worth reporting even when the cupboard is dry. Note whether the sealant has separated from one edge, cracked through the bead or become detached in several places. The assessor needs to inspect the surfaces and joint, including any movement or damaged edges that could affect a replacement seal.

Painting over a split or spreading a new bead across loose material does not answer why the joint opened. The repair scope should identify removal of failed material, preparation of the exposed joint and the sealant suitable for its materials and movement. If a splashback or benchtop is loose, address that condition as part of the plan before treating the gap as a routine cosmetic repair.

Kitchen corners and sink edges are separate details. Ask which joints are included rather than assuming that “kitchen resealing” covers every junction in the room. A marked photograph or a written list can make the boundary clear.

Sealant compatibility, preparation and curing

Sealant choice depends on the actual benchtop, sink and splashback materials. Stone, laminate, metal and coated surfaces should not be treated as interchangeable. For example, Sika’s Sikasil-702 product information includes kitchen and splashback uses but excludes natural stone and certain other materials. A kitchen label alone does not establish compatibility with your installation.

Ask how the joint will be prepared and whether a manufacturer-specified pretreatment or a different product is needed. Sika’s guidance for that product calls for a sound, clean and dry substrate; primer is not a substitute for poor preparation. This is a product example, not a declaration that MEL ONE uses it on every kitchen.

Agree return-to-use instructions for the selected sealant. Sika’s curing guidance describes moisture curing and the influence of humidity and temperature. Surface skinning is not the same as full cure through a joint. Avoid assuming one fixed number of hours for every bead, product and Canberra season.

Domestic kitchen sealing and bathroom waterproofing requirements

The ABCB waterproofing FAQ excludes domestic food preparation areas from its wet-area definition. A residential kitchen therefore should not automatically receive the same waterproofing scope as a bathroom. This does not establish that a particular kitchen project has no requirements: its use, construction and applicable ACT rules still need checking.

For a sink-edge leak, ask for the actual junction work and any associated damage to be identified. For a wider renovation, describe changes to the floor, plumbing, cabinetry and adjoining areas before accepting a proposal. Do not use a bathroom membrane quotation as a template without checking whether the work addresses the kitchen’s confirmed problem.

The guide to regrouting, resealing and rewaterproofing explains the distinction between surface joint work and renewal of a concealed waterproofing system. Neither category replaces the need to find a plumbing leak.

A clear kitchen repair enquiry and written scope

Start with your Canberra or ACT address, the affected sink or junction and when moisture appears. Mention a swollen cupboard, previous sealing work and any appliance connection nearby. For a rented property, notify the property manager and agree who can authorise work. For strata property, identify any access or approval arrangements relevant to the affected area.

A useful scope names the joints to reseal, preparation and materials, plumbing tasks requiring separate attention, and whether cabinet repairs or reinstatement are included. If opening the area reveals more damage, ask for the finding, proposed extra work and its effect on cost and use of the kitchen before approving the change.

Arrange access to the sink and cupboard without dismantling anything yourself. Confirm when the repaired joint can be exposed to water and what to report if moisture returns. Describe your kitchen leak to MEL ONE; optional photographs can support the enquiry, and the on-site assessment establishes the repair plan.

Related waterproofing questions

Can sink resealing fix a wet cupboard?

It can address a confirmed leak through an accessible sink perimeter or adjoining joint. A leaking supply, waste or appliance connection needs its own assessment. The wet cupboard alone does not tell you which repair is suitable.

Should I replace a swollen cabinet board before finding the leak?

Find and address the moisture source before arranging restoration. Resealing does not reverse swelling or restore the strength of damaged board. Ask for source repair and cabinet restoration to be described separately.

Does a split splashback joint mean the kitchen needs bathroom-style waterproofing?

No. The joint and surrounding materials need assessment. Domestic kitchens are excluded from the NCC wet-area definition described in the ABCB FAQ, and the particular construction and ACT project requirements still need checking.
