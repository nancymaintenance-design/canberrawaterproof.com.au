Is water under the sink coming from the edge or a pipe?

It can come from either, so the location of a wet cupboard is only a starting point. Water appearing after splashes on the benchtop may point towards a sink-edge joint. Water appearing around a tap connection, trap or waste pipe needs plumbing assessment. Describe when the cupboard gets wet and where you first notice water without dismantling connections to investigate.

If an accessible sink perimeter or splashback-to-benchtop joint is the confirmed issue, the sealing scope can identify the old sealant to remove, the surfaces to prepare and the joints to reseal. The sink resealing or plumbing guide helps explain which details to report.

Will resealing restore a swollen kitchen cabinet?

No. Resealing a confirmed joint leak addresses that water path; it does not restore a swollen or softened cabinet board. Find and address the moisture source before planning replacement or other cabinet repairs. Ask whether damage around the joint affects the sealing work, and have any cabinet restoration identified separately. Describe the sink, cupboard and timing to discuss the appropriate assessment. A kitchen sealing quote should specify its actual junction repairs rather than assume the same scope as a bathroom renovation.
