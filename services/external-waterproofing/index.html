What should be checked when a window leaks in wind-driven rain?

The window joints, frame drainage and surrounding wall details may all be relevant. Tell us which side gets wet first and whether the problem occurs only with a particular wind direction. Water visible beside the frame can originate elsewhere, so the symptom alone does not justify sealing every opening or coating the wall. Dampness that also appears without rain may need a different investigation.

Does rainwater under a back door need more silicone?

Not necessarily. The threshold, adjoining joints, outside surface levels and drainage need consideration. Describe whether water reaches the doorway as surface runoff or appears through a particular gap. A local seal is useful only where it addresses an identified joint defect; threshold or drainage work may need a separate scope.

What if water pools against the side wall?

Ask for drainage and surface falls to be considered alongside any water-entry detail. A coating alone may leave the source of pooled water unresolved. The waterproofing or drainage guide helps frame the discussion. For water appearing at the eaves, start with the roof leak service. Otherwise, report the rain pattern and affected area so the appropriate assessment can be discussed.
