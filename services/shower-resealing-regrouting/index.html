Shower grout and silicone repair suitability

We check missing grout between tiles, split silicone at changes of plane, screen junctions and signs of tile movement. Old silicone, residue and damp joint surfaces affect preparation and the bond of the replacement sealant.

Grout fills tile joints; flexible sealant serves movement joints. Neither treatment repairs a concealed membrane failure or a plumbing leak. Persistent moisture after a previous surface repair needs reassessment before more grout or silicone is applied.

Grout and silicone repair scope

Our quote identifies the joints being treated, grout removal depth, preparation and replacement materials. If we find loose tiles or a damaged substrate, we explain the separate repairs needed before regrouting.

  • Inspection of grout, silicone, screen edges and other accessible shower junctions.
  • Removal of failed sealant or grout from the defined repair area.
  • New grout, silicone and agreed finishing work, or a clear recommendation when broader waterproofing is required.

Shower joint preparation, curing and reuse

  1. Identify the worn grout or silicone joints and describe any leak that continued after earlier repairs.
  2. Agree removal of old silicone or deteriorated grout, cleaning and drying before the selected repair.
  3. Keep the shower out of use for the specified preparation and curing periods, then follow the written handover instructions.

Grout, sealant and bond breakers are not interchangeable

Grout fills appropriate fixed tile joints. Flexible sealants and bond breakers form part of specified movement-joint or membrane details; they are not substitutes for grout across a tiled floor. Product compatibility and the condition found on site determine the choice. See tile regrouting and epoxy grout options for tiled areas beyond the shower, or the Sika waterproofing guidance for the role of bond breakers.
