Balcony ponding, drainage and membrane condition

Ponding describes water remaining on the surface; it does not establish where a leak starts. Drainage outlets, surface falls, thresholds and waterproofing junctions may need assessment together. Tell us where water collects, how long it remains after rain and whether staining appears below. A drainage issue and a waterproofing defect can require different work, even when both symptoms occur.

The balcony leak guide explains why the visible stain may not identify the entry point. Where access to the waterproofing layer is needed, ask the written scope to distinguish tile removal, substrate work, membrane details and retiling from any separate drainage work.

Balcony leak records for strata managers

Share the dates and weather conditions, the affected locations above and below, any safely taken photographs and records of previous repairs. Identify who can arrange access to each area and ask what approval is required before an inspection or work begins. These records support the discussion; they do not establish responsibility for the defect or its cost. Contact MEL ONE about the balcony with the available information and any access or approval constraints so the proposed assessment can be discussed.

Balcony inspection and membrane repair paths

Accessible outlets, door thresholds, perimeter upturns, tile joints and the underside of the balcony are checked against the reported rain pattern. Ponding and staining help locate concerns but do not establish the complete concealed membrane condition.

An isolated junction may allow a local repair where the existing system can be reliably joined. Broader failure may require tile removal, substrate preparation, membrane renewal and retiling. Regrouting alone does not renew a balcony membrane; drainage alterations and structural repairs need their own scope.

Balcony repair scope

The written quote defines removal, membrane terminations, outlet and threshold details, finishes and any separate drainage tasks. Strata approval and access to the room below may be needed before work can be arranged.

  • Assessment of accessible tiled surfaces, door thresholds, drainage points and external junctions.
  • Preparation of the agreed balcony repair area before membrane or sealant work.
  • The Ellis team completes the listed waterproofing, retiling and finishing work; any separate drainage task is identified clearly.

Waterproofing repair and access arrangements

  1. Describe rain-related symptoms, ponding and the rooms affected below the balcony.
  2. Arrange balcony and downstairs access, identify strata authority and confirm the repair or renewal boundary.
  3. Agree weather planning, curing, temporary access restrictions and reinstatement in the written quote.

Balcony regrouting and membrane replacement boundaries

MEL ONE offers standalone balcony regrouting, including epoxy grout where suitable. It can be an option for worn grout when the tiles and underlying construction are sound. Loose tiles, recurring leaks or moisture below need assessment before choosing a grout-only repair. Regrouting does not replace a failed waterproofing membrane.

Balcony regrouting and waterproofing cost factors

The area, old grout removal, material choice and access affect the quote. A grout-only repair is a different scope from tile removal, membrane renewal and retiling. Tell us whether you need grout repairs or have an active leak; our waterproofing quote checklist explains which inclusions to compare.
