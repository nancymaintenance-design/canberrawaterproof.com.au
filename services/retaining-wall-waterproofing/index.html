Retaining wall moisture and access assessment

For retaining-wall moisture, we assess accessible wall joints, cracks, adjoining rooms, ground levels and drainage together. On the soil side, suitable bitumen-rubber, sheet or cementitious systems may be considered with their required protection. For treatment from the inside, we assess a system specifically documented for the substrate and negative-side water pressure. Access and the direction of the water pressure are central to the material selection.

Retaining-wall preparation and detailing usually cover the soundness of the mineral substrate, repairs, specified priming, wall joints and terminations. We confirm curing, any agreed inspection and protection before reinstatement or backfilling. Drainage design and any excavation support or structural movement are reviewed with the appropriate specialists, so the waterproofing and related work have clear responsibilities in the quote. Waterproofing materials and installation guide

Garage water entry beside a retaining wall after rain

Water at a garage wall can follow soil moisture, surface runoff, drainage defects or a wall junction. The visible damp patch alone cannot establish the source or whether pressure is acting behind the wall. The Ellis team reviews the rain pattern and accessible wall and drainage details during an on-site assessment, then confirms the repair plan and written quote.

Retaining wall access and internal treatment limitations

The repair side and method depend on how the wall was built and whether the soil-facing surface can be reached. An internal coating is not automatically equivalent to addressing water on the retained side. Where excavation, drainage design or structural movement is involved, the relevant specialist input should be included in the agreed scope.

Retaining wall repair scope

Our retaining-wall quote identifies the wall face we can access, preparation, waterproofing terminations and the reinstatement boundary. Excavation, drainage and structural tasks are listed separately from membrane work.

  • Inspection of accessible wall faces, adjoining ground levels, drainage outlets and the timing of moisture after rainfall.
  • The Ellis team checks access, excavation and drainage conditions before defining the waterproofing scope; any specialist structural task is listed separately.
  • Preparation, waterproofing and protection work only for the retaining-wall area confirmed in the written scope.

Retaining wall access and waterproofing sequence

  1. Describe rain-related seepage, adjoining rooms and visible wall changes without disturbing the backfill.
  2. Arrange an on-site assessment of accessible faces and discuss soil-side access, buried services and excavation constraints.
  3. Agree the written quote and any structural, drainage or excavation responsibilities before work starts.
