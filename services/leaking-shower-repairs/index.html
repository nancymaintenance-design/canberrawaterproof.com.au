Carpet, paint and ceiling moisture after shower use

Wet carpet beside the shower, bubbling paint on the wall behind it or a ceiling drip after an upstairs shower are reasons to arrange an assessment. These signs alone do not identify a failed membrane. Water may escape through a screen edge or junction, or come from a supply or waste connection. A plumbing fault needs a plumber; a waterproofing repair should follow the assessed water path.

Tell us where the first moisture appears, whether it follows every shower and whether it also occurs when the shower is unused. Existing photos and a description of earlier repairs can help. Our shower plumbing or waterproofing guide explains the different repair paths.

Recurring shower leaks after regrouting or resealing

A surface repair may not have addressed the source, or another detail may need attention. Before paying for more grout or silicone, describe what was repaired and whether the symptom changed. The assessment can then consider those details and whether access beneath finishes is needed. Bathroom waterproofing and retiling is the route for an agreed membrane renewal scope; contact MEL ONE if the cause is still unclear.

Shower leak inspection and repair options

The inspection considers the shower base, screen edges, wall-to-floor junctions, floor waste and finishes in the adjacent room. When moisture appears after use, we consider surface escape as well as concealed paths; a stain alone cannot identify a failed membrane.

A local screen-edge or joint repair may suit an isolated defect. Recurring leaks, loose tiles or damage beneath finishes may need access for membrane renewal and retiling. Supply or waste pipe faults require a plumber and are identified separately in the written quote.

Shower repair scope

The shower repair scope identifies the affected detail, any tile removal and the extent of reinstatement. Keep the shower available for inspection and tell us if it is the only shower in use.

  • Assessment of the shower base, screen edges, tiled joints and nearby finishes.
  • Removal and preparation work where access is needed to the affected waterproofing detail.
  • Agreed waterproofing, tiling, grout, silicone and finishing work for the defined repair path.

Waterproofing repair and access arrangements

  1. Record whether moisture follows shower use, appears while unused or reaches the adjacent room.
  2. Arrange an on-site assessment of the shower and adjoining finishes; agree any further access before removal.
  3. Confirm the written quote, repair boundaries and shower downtime, including curing and return-to-use instructions.
